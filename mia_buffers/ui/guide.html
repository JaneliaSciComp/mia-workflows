Training on a MIA buffer

A buffer is a pool of training crops drawn from the catalogued datasets that match its definition. The definition is a short YAML text, and it describes both ends of the buffer. On the input side it says which datasets qualify. On the output side it says how crops are presented to the model: the patch size and pyramid levels, the channels, the normalisation, the weighting between datasets, and how volumes are split into train, validation and test. The system plans the buffer over the datasets it already knows how to read, downloads and converts them, and serves crop descriptors over HTTP; a Python dataset class turns them into tensors in the training process.

A buffer is six steps on one page, in a fixed order, and its page opens at the step it is on. Each step has one button that moves it forward and one that goes back; what else can be asked of it is under Actions…. Every step the system takes is an entry in the buffer's timeline, under the step. Needs you lists whatever is waiting for a person. Something that can wait is taken off that list with Shelve… and a reason: nothing starts it and no plan takes it until Unshelve. The page needs no login: anyone who can reach it can press its buttons.

The whole loop

Step 1. Define

New buffer, on the Buffers tab, opens a buffer that is not saved yet. Its definition has two tabs over one text. Controls sets it through filters: dimensionality, licence, annotation source, whether annotations are required, the smallest array accepted, the patch size and pyramid levels, the weighting between datasets, the crops per split, the most crops one dataset may hold, and the datasets held out for test. YAML shows the same definition as text: edit or paste there and the controls follow. When the YAML holds something no control shows, the YAML is the definition, the page says what the controls cannot show, and the controls are read-only while it holds that. YAML that is not a definition is said so beside the tabs, and the way forward is closed until it is one.

The name is a label. It need not be unique and nothing looks a buffer up by it. sampling.crops is the buffer's size: how many distinct crops it holds per split, shared out among the datasets by weight and placed at random in each. The val and test lists are drawn from their crops and are that long, or shorter when the datasets hold fewer.

Under the definition are the numbers of its plan, computed on the server at each change: how many datasets fit, how many of those are prepared, the weight share by modality and the heaviest datasets. The weighting temperature runs from uniform over modalities (0) to proportional to each dataset's capacity (1).

The fields of a definition that are usually edited:

name: my-2d-nuclei                      # a label: it may repeat, and can be changed
catalog: {repo: …, ref: <commit>}       # pinned by the page: the plan is reproducible
select:
  where:
    dims: {in: [2D]}
    modality: {in: [fluorescence, brightfield]}
    license: {in: [CC0-1.0, CC-BY-4.0], allow_unknown: false}
    annotations: {required: true}
  exclude: {ids: [a-dataset-id]}        # the datasets unchecked on step 2
sampling:
  patch: {2D: {y: 256, x: 256}, c: 1}   # crop edges in pixels at level 1; one channel per crop
  levels: [1, 2, 4]                     # the pyramid levels each crop is served at
  weighting: {scheme: temperature, over: [modality, dataset], temperature: 0.5}
  normalize: {method: percentile, range: [1, 99], scope: volume}
splits:
  fractions: {train: 0.8, val: 0.1, test: 0.1}
ready:
  min_fraction: 0.5                     # how much of each weighting group must be servable before the buffer counts as ready

Step 2. Datasets

Next lists the datasets that fit the definition, the prepared ones first: the same number step 1 counted. Where datasets leave counts the datasets each rule removes; the last rule, not prepared, means a dataset fits but no reader exists for its files yet, and the other rules are the definition's own filters, its patch (a footprint larger than the dataset) and what a record lacks (no array shape, no voxel size). A dataset the patch does not fit is listed when you check also list the datasets that do not fit. A dataset whose record lacks something is never listed in a buffer: the step counts them and links to the Datasets tab, which flags what each record lacks (no array shape, no voxel size, no data URL) and filters by it, or by nothing for the records that are whole. Uncheck a dataset to take it out of this buffer: it is listed above the table, with put back, and written to the definition as an excluded id.

Save buffer and continue creates the buffer. Nothing runs yet. From here on the buffer has a page of its own, which the Buffers tab lists, and its definition can still be changed: go back to step 1 or 2, change it, and going on saves the change. A definition can be changed while the buffer rests: before its first plan, and after a round that was cancelled, stopped or retired. While a round is in progress it cannot (go back from that step, which cancels the round), and a buffer that serves is fixed. Clone, under Actions…, opens New buffer on a copy of any buffer's definition.

Step 3. Prepare

A buffer is planned over prepared datasets only. This step lists every dataset that fits, with the columns of the Datasets tab. Sort by stage to bring the datasets of one stage together and check the ones you want (the header's check takes the whole page): a box then says how many are selected, and its two buttons each file one preparation request for this buffer. Resume runs what is left: a dataset that never ran starts (an agent run that writes and verifies a reader for that dataset's files; some stop with a question for a person), one that stopped goes on from the step that failed with everything before it kept, one whose crops wait at review keeps them, and one that is prepared is left alone. Restart prepares every checked dataset from scratch on today's catalog, readers and harness, a prepared one too, and one whose crops wait at review starts over without them. Either leaves a dataset alone whose round is running. Use Restart when something a round is pinned to was fixed: Resume keeps the versions the round began with, so a step that failed because of a fault in them fails again. The step lists the buffer's requests with their stage, and each dataset with where it stands. (To prepare datasets apart from any buffer, check them on the Datasets tab; every request is listed under Preparation requests there.)

The buffer waits at this step for as long as you like, and nothing about it runs meanwhile. Continue with N prepared datasets plans it over whatever is prepared at that moment. Datasets that are prepared later join the next plan.

Step 4. Plan

The round resolves the definition against the catalog and the prepared datasets and stops at its plan: the datasets in this version, one row each, with weights and crops. Nothing has been downloaded. Run: fetch and stage accepts the plan; before it starts, the page lists any host the downloads need that jobs may not reach yet, to approve with the start. Change the buffer, the way back, cancels the round, and the buffer rests at step 3 where it can be changed and planned again.

Step 5. Source

The datasets of the plan are downloaded and converted, several at a time, and the step shows each one: on disk, fetching, staging, waiting, or failed with the reason. Sourcing runs until no dataset has a job left; the buffer is then staged, and its timeline gets the board: each dataset's outcome, each weighting group's servable share against ready.min_fraction, volumes per split, and bytes on disk.

Approve and serve serves the version as the step shows it. A dataset that failed stays out; one that is waiting keeps its place and joins when it lands. The share each group has at the approve becomes the floor it is judged against afterwards, so a partial buffer accepted on purpose is not later reported as degraded. The way back cancels the round, as on step 4. Converted pyramids stay in the shared cache, so a later round over the same datasets skips the conversion.

  • Waiting on a drop. Some datasets sit on hosts no job may reach. The timeline lists the files to place by hand in the dataset's incoming/ directory. Once the copy has finished, press Files are in place on this step, and the worker takes them into the cache. The button is refused while the directory is empty or a file in it changed in the last minute.
  • Waiting for a quota or a lock. The cache is full, or another buffer is converting the same dataset. Both clear on their own.
  • Day limit. A group with nothing servable after sourcing.dataset_timeout_days (default 14) drops its waiters; with nothing servable at all the buffer stops at needs-human.
  • Automatic approve. plan: {auto_approve: true} in the definition approves the staged version without a person when every group meets min_fraction. A short group still waits for one.

Step 6. Serve

The step names the version being served by its id, with the line a training script pastes. Every round that reaches its plan makes a version with an id of its own, and a training run names that id, never the buffer's name. The step lists the buffer's versions, each opening a page with its events, datasets, volumes, consumers and usage.

A serving buffer's definition is fixed. Plan a new version plans it again over the datasets that are prepared now; the version being served keeps serving the runs connected to it until the new one is approved. Revoke the checked withdraws single datasets from the serving version, and Retire, under Actions…, releases all of it.

Training

Step 6 names the id of the version being served. This cell runs in the harness environment of the mia-snippets checkout, with this repository on the Python path and torch and muvit installed:

from mia_buffers.client import BufferDataset

URL, TENSORIZERS = "<service url>", "<mia-snippets checkout>"   # or export MIA_BUFFERS_TENSORIZERS
train_ds = BufferDataset(URL, "<buffer id>", "train", n_samples=4000, tensorizers=TENSORIZERS)
val_ds = BufferDataset(URL, "<buffer id>", "val", tensorizers=TENSORIZERS)

loader = DataLoader(train_ds, batch_size=32, num_workers=8, collate_fn=BufferDataset.collate)
model = MuViTMAE2d(in_channels=train_ds.n_channels, levels=train_ds.levels, ...)
  • Train is a stream. Each item is a crop drawn by the service under the definition's weighting; n_samples is the length of one virtual epoch and the index is ignored. Val and test replay a frozen list that is the same every epoch and every run.
  • The service serves descriptors, not pixels. A descriptor names the pyramid, the window, the channels and the normalisation bounds. The training process reads the OME-Zarr pyramid from shared storage and tensorises it, so the load of a DataLoader with many workers falls on the training node. Each worker registers itself as a consumer, listed on the buffer's page.
  • What a sample holds. The image at each level in sampling.levels, the label layers the dataset has, and meta with the record, the voxel size and the crop's position in physical units. A buffer mixes datasets whose label layers differ, which is why the custom collate is needed: a batch keeps the keys every sample has.
  • What is pinned. A version records the catalog commit, the definition's hash, the plan hash and the commit of the conversion code. A checkout at a different commit raises on construction; allow_recipe_mismatch=True turns that into a warning.
  • Before the approve, while the buffer needs a human, or when it has no approved event, the service answers 409 with the reason and the client raises BufferNotApproved. After a retire it answers 410. A split with nothing servable yet raises BufferEmpty.

During training, and afterwards

The Buffers tab lists every buffer with its step and the id it is serving; a version's own page has its datasets, the volumes per split, the consumers seen, the draws per day and a health line. After ready the buffer changes only when one of these happens:

  • Degraded. When a group's servable share falls under the floor set at the approve (a pyramid was evicted or lost), the timeline says so and the worker stages the missing datasets again from the cache.
  • Idle. No consumer for idle.hours (default 72) moves the buffer to idle; Keep returns it to ready. After retire_after_days (default 14) more days without a draw the keeper retires the version and its space is reclaimed.
  • Revoked. Revoke the checked removes single datasets from the serving version; Retire releases all of it.
  • A new version. Plan a new version builds one with a new id, reusing what is in the cache. A training run that should use it is given the new id.

Its actions are in a timeline as cleaner.
